EEG quality assurance

EEG Quality Control Workflow

A practical path from raw recordings to analysis-ready EEG. Quality control is not a single cleaning step: inspect the data, make an explicit decision, apply the least destructive response, and check the result again before analysis.

The central rule

Do not ask only, “Can this artifact be removed?” Ask whether it matters for the planned analysis, whether the correction may remove neural signal, and whether enough comparable data remain in every condition.

How to use the workflow

Four quality-control stages

1

Before preprocessing

Confirm data integrity and inspect the continuous recording

Load the raw EEG together with channel information, sampling rate, event markers, reference information, and acquisition notes. Confirm that the recording is complete before changing it.

  • Check channel names, types, locations, reference, and sampling rate.
  • Confirm event codes, timing, recording duration, and missing segments.
  • Scroll through continuous data at more than one time scale.
  • Identify persistently poor-quality channels before running ICA.
Decision: Are any channels clearly poor quality? If yes, mark them and decide whether removal or interpolation is justified. Keep a record of the original channel status.
2

Dataset-level check

Assess global and systematic noise

Look beyond individual electrodes. Line noise, reference problems, clipping, drift, cable movement, and widespread contamination can make an entire segment or recording unsuitable.

  • Inspect amplitude ranges, spectra, line noise, drift, and clipping.
  • Check whether noise is brief, continuous, or condition-related.
  • Compare affected periods with task events and participant responses.
Decision: Is data quality recoverable without distorting the signal of interest?
If not: repair only when defensible; otherwise restrict the affected analysis or exclude the segment, run, or participant using criteria defined in advance.
3

Preprocess and characterize

Identify artifacts, then choose a response based on impact

Apply and review preprocessing in a documented order. Filtering, re-referencing, resampling, ICA, regression, rejection, and interpolation all change the data and should not be treated as automatic improvements.

  • Characterize ocular, muscle, cardiac, electrode, and physiological artifacts.
  • Ask whether each artifact overlaps the signal, condition, or time window of interest.
  • Compare data before and after every consequential correction.
If the artifact has a meaningful impact: choose a specific strategy - correct, annotate, reject, or retain - and state why its assumptions fit this dataset.

4

Analysis readiness

Validate the final data and document the decision

Quality is analysis-specific. Review continuous data, epochs, event counts, spectra, ERP waveforms, topographies, or other outputs that match the planned inference.

  • Confirm enough usable trials or time remain in every condition.
  • Check that exclusions are not systematically different across conditions or groups.
  • Verify that preprocessing did not introduce edge effects, distortions, or implausible topographies.
  • Record thresholds, rejected data, interpolated channels, uncertainty, and retention decisions.
Final validation: proceed only when signal, topography, spectra, ERP measures, or other planned outputs are interpretable and the remaining dataset can answer the question.

Practical documents

Use a checklist while you review

The workflow tells you where decisions occur; the checklists help you record what was inspected. Complete the collection checklist at acquisition and the processing checklist whenever a major preprocessing decision is made.

During acquisition Data collection checklist Setup, recording quality, events, participant notes, and metadata. During processing Data processing checklist Channels, noise, artifacts, preprocessing decisions, and final validation.

Document decisions, not only settings

A reproducible QC log should state what was observed, the rule used to make the decision, what was changed, and what uncertainty remains. Software settings alone do not explain why data were retained or removed.
